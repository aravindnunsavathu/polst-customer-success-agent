# **The one structural fact that shapes everything: ****Polst**** prices per campaign**

Polst charges by number of campaigns, not by seat. This is consumption pricing, and it changes the shape of the CS function in five specific ways. Every document in this pack assumes it.

## **1. Retention and expansion are the same measurement**

Under seat-based pricing, retention (keep the seats) and expansion (sell more seats) are genuinely different motions with different owners. Under consumption pricing they are one continuous variable: **campaign volume over time.** Volume going up is expansion. Volume going down is churn in progress. There is no clean line between them.

## **2. Your revenue has a driver tree, and it is your expansion strategy**

Revenue per account

  = Departments live

  × Active campaign creators per department

  × Campaigns per creator per period

  × Price per campaign

Each of the first three terms is a distinct CS motion:

- **Departments live** → land-and-expand. Closest to a sales motion. Different skills; consider whether CS or Sales owns it.

- **Creators per department** → seeding and enablement. Single-threaded usage (one power user per department) is your single biggest fragility. When that person leaves, the department's volume goes to zero.

- **Campaigns per creator** → depth of use and value realization. Creators run more campaigns when the last campaign worked.

Effective campaigns are what makes a creator run the next one. Treat campaign quality as a leading indicator of volume, not as a separate goal.

## **3. Churn is decay, not an event**

Logo retention will flatter you. An account that drops from 40 campaigns/quarter to 8 has churned 80% of its value while remaining a customer in good standing. Two consequences:

- **Your primary retention metric is Net Revenue Retention (NRR), not logo retention.** Track logo retention too, but never lead with it.

- **Your churn alarm is a volume trend, not a renewal date.** A 90-day rolling volume decline against an account's own trailing baseline should trigger intervention long before anyone mentions a contract.

## **4. Revenue is unforecastable unless you convert usage into commitment**

Pure pay-as-you-go volume gives you no floor and no visibility. A high-leverage CS motion under this model is moving accounts from ad-hoc consumption to **committed annual campaign volume** (prepaid blocks, annual commitments with tiered pricing, or minimums), in exchange for a unit-price discount.

This is worth flagging to your CEO/CRO explicitly — it is a pricing/packaging decision, not purely a CS decision, but CS is the function that will execute it. It belongs in the Charter conversation (doc 01).

**Watch item:** if you introduce commitments, health scoring must also watch *commitment utilization*. An account that prepaid 100 campaigns and has used 12 with two months left is a renewal risk wearing a healthy revenue number.

## **5. There is a perverse incentive to guard against**

If CS is measured on campaign volume, the fastest path to the number is pushing customers to run campaigns they do not need. That works for one or two quarters and then collapses, because campaigns that produce no result do not get repeated.

Guard against it by pairing every volume metric with a quality/outcome metric in the same review. Specific guardrails are built into doc 02.

# **Adjusted outcome framework**

**Root outcome (new): The customer achieves the business result they bought ****Polst**** to achieve.**

Retention and expansion are lagging indicators of this. Making it the root changes what CSMs actually do — it pushes them toward "what were you trying to accomplish, and did it happen" rather than "your renewal is in 60 days."

| **Level** | **Outcome** | **Sub-outcomes** | **Primary metric** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Root** | Customer achieves their business result | — | % of accounts with a documented, customer-confirmed result in last 2 quarters |
| **1** | **Revenue retained and grown** |  | **NRR** |
| 1a | Successful onboarding to first value | Customer can use the product *and* knows what value looks like | Time to first completed campaign; time to first confirmed result |
| 1b | Effective early use | Guided, nudged use through first ~90 days | Campaigns in first 90 days vs. target |
| 1c | Sustained, self-sufficient, effective use | Merged with old 2(b) and 2(c) — volume *and* quality | 90-day campaign volume trend; campaign outcome rate |
| 1d | **Stakeholder coverage** *(new)* | Multi-threaded relationships; named economic buyer; exec sponsor who can articulate why Polst matters | % accounts with ≥3 mapped contacts and a named exec sponsor |
| 1e | **Documented value realization** *(new)* | Written, customer-agreed account of the result Polst delivered | % accounts with value doc refreshed in last 2 quarters |
| **2** | **Footprint expanded** |  | **Departments live; creators per department** |
| 2a | More departments live | Land-and-expand motion | Net new departments per quarter |
| 2b | More creators per department *(new — closes single-threading risk)* | Seeding and enablement | % departments with ≥2 active creators |
| **3** | **Voice of customer flows back** *(new)* | Churn/decay reason capture; product feedback routing; defined Sales→CS handoff | Coverage: % of decay events with a logged reason |

## **Why the additions matter**

**1d — Stakeholder coverage.** Usage is necessary but not sufficient. Accounts are lost for reasons usage data never predicts: the champion leaves, the exec sponsor never really existed, budget consolidates, a competitor bundles the capability in. Under consumption pricing this is sharper still — a single departing power user can zero out a department's volume overnight.

**1e — Documented value realization.** Without it, every commercial conversation is an assertion. With it, you have evidence. It is also the artifact that survives champion turnover.

**3 — Voice of customer.** You will be the only function with continuous post-sale contact. Nobody else will build churn-reason capture, product feedback routing, or a written definition of what a good closed-won handoff from Sales looks like. These are cheap now and expensive to retrofit.
