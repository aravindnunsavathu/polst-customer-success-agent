**Segmentation ****&**** Coverage Model**

**Purpose:** decide which customers get which level of attention, before the answer defaults to "whoever emailed most recently."

**Owner:** VP Customer Success · **Approver:** CEO

**Version:** 0.1

# **1. Why this is the highest-stakes decision you will make as first CS hire**

The coverage model is simply the honest allocation of CS personnel hours. It also determines what the eventual playbook contains. A high-touch playbook and a tech-touch playbook share almost no plays.

# **2. Segment on potential, not just current spend**

The standard mistake is to tier by current revenue. Under per-campaign pricing this is especially wrong, because current volume reflects *how far along the adoption curve an account is*, not how big it can get. A 3-department account running 5 campaigns/quarter may be worth ten times a 1-department account running 20.

**Recommended axes:**

- **Potential value** — realistic campaign volume at full adoption: addressable departments × expected creators × expected campaigns per creator. Estimate this at onboarding and record it in the account plan.

- **Current reali****z****ation** — current volume ÷ potential volume.

|  | **Low reali****z****ation** | **High reali****z****ation** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High potential** | **Invest** — biggest NRR upside in the portfolio. Highest touch. | **Protect** — your reference accounts and revenue base. High touch, expansion-oriented. |
| **Low potential** | **Qualify or automate** — cheap programmatic help; do not spend a CSM here | **Maintain** — efficient, automated, low-touch. Farm for advocacy. |

A pure revenue tiering hides the Invest quadrant entirely, and that quadrant is where a first CS hire generates the most measurable value.

# **3. Proposed tiers**

Fill the thresholds from your actual book — these placeholders assume mid-market/enterprise (Assumption A6).

| **Tier** | **Definition** | **Approx. % of accounts** | **Coverage** | **Named CSM** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tier 1 — Strategic** | Potential ≥ [$X] ARR-equivalent, or ≥[N] addressable departments, or strategically important (logo, reference, design partner) | [10–15]% | High touch | Yes |
| **Tier 2 — Growth** | Potential ≥ [$Y], multi-department, expansion path visible | [25–35]% | Mid touch / pooled | Pooled |
| **Tier 3 — Scale** | Everything else with viable economics | [50–60]% | Tech touch / digital | No |
| **Tier 4 — Review** | Below the profitable-to-serve floor (doc 01 §5) | [<5]% | Automated only; decide whether to serve | No |

**Re-tier quarterly.** Tiering is a hypothesis about potential, and potential estimates are wrong. An account that moves from Invest to Protect should drop touch level; one that reveals unexpected footprint should rise.

# **4. What each tier actually gets**

| **Activity** | **T1 Strategic** | **T2 Growth** | **T3 Scale** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kickoff | Live, with exec sponsor, custom success criteria | Live, standard agenda | Automated + group onboarding webinar |
| Onboarding | Bespoke plan, weekly for 30 days | Standard plan, 2 checkpoints | In-product + email sequence |
| Regular contact | Monthly call | Quarterly call | Automated; on-demand |
| Business review | Quarterly, exec-level, value doc refresh | Semi-annual, lighter | Annual automated summary |
| Value doc | Maintained, customer-confirmed | Maintained, CS-authored | Auto-generated usage summary |
| Account plan | Full, refreshed quarterly | Light, refreshed semi-annually | None |
| Health monitoring | Weekly | Bi-weekly | Automated alert only |
| Decay intervention | Personal, within 3 days | Personal, within 10 days | Automated sequence, escalates to human if unresolved |
| Expansion motion | Proactive, planned | Triggered by signal | Self-serve / in-product |
| Escalation path | Direct to VP CS + exec sponsor | VP CS | Support queue |

**The tech-touch tier is not "no service" — it is *producti**z**ed* service.** It needs real assets (an onboarding email sequence, in-product guides, a webinar, a help center) that mostly do not exist yet at a company this stage. Building them is a project with a real cost. Budget for it, or be honest that Tier 3 is currently unserved.

| **Available hours** | [1,400] |
| **Gap** |  |

If the gap is negative, you have exactly four levers. Pick explicitly rather than absorbing the gap through overwork:

- Reduce Tier 1 count (raise the bar)

- Reduce hours per account (productize more)

- Add headcount

- Accept lower coverage on a named tier — **and say so in writing**

**Rough benchmark for the hiring case:** high-touch CSMs typically carry [$1–2M] ARR or [8–15] strategic accounts; mid-touch [20–50] accounts; tech-touch [100–500+]. Treat these as orders of magnitude, not targets.

# **6. Triggers that override tier**

Certain conditions warrant temporary high touch regardless of tier. Time-box each so an account does not stay elevated indefinitely.

| **Trigger** | **Response** | **Duration** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 90 days, any tier ≥T2 | Elevate one level | 90 days |
| Health Critical | Elevate to T1 touch | Until Watch or exit |
| Champion departure detected | Immediate human contact | 30 days |
| New department launching | Elevate one level | Until first value |
| Commitment renewal within 90 days | Elevate one level | Through renewal |
| Reference / case study candidate | Elevate one level | Duration of project |

# **7. What to build first for the low-touch tiers**

Ranked by leverage per hour of build effort:

- **Onboarding email sequence** tied to actual milestones (not day counts) — highest leverage, lowest cost

- **A "your first campaign" guide** — the single biggest drop-off point

- **Decay alert → automated re-engagement sequence**

- **Group onboarding webinar**, recorded and reused

- **Help cent****e****r covering the top 10 support tickets** — instrument which tickets those are first

- **Automated quarterly usage summary** emailed to the account's economic buyer

Items 1–3 cover most of the value. Do not build 4–6 until 1–3 are working.
